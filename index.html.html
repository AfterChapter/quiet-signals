Listening for the Quiet Signals

A Self-Assessment for Women in Transition

Most transitions do not arrive without warning. They arrive in stages — first as a whisper you can almost ignore, then as something louder you can no longer pretend you did not hear. This assessment helps you identify where you are in that arc.

◎

How to use this: Read each statement below. Check every statement that feels true for you — even partially true. When you are done, click See My Results to find your stage.

Identity Signals

These statements surface how clearly you can locate yourself outside of your role.

I cannot answer the question "tell me about yourself" without defaulting to work or family as the predominant part of my answer.
My sense of purpose feels closely tied to my job title or organizational affiliation.
When I meet someone new, I feel uncomfortable or at a loss if I cannot lead with what I do professionally.
I have described myself as "between things" or "figuring out my next move" for longer than feels comfortable.
When I try to describe what I value most, I notice I am describing what I have done, not who I am.
I feel a quiet grief about a version of myself I have not fully acknowledged losing.
I find it difficult to make decisions about my future without knowing what my title or role will be.
I measure my progress or worth by metrics that no longer feel meaningful to me.
I have caught myself performing confidence or certainty I do not actually feel.
The question "what do you want?" — not "what are you qualified for?" — genuinely stumps me.
Environmental Signals

These statements surface how your environment has been working on you — often below the surface.

I have felt consistently underestimated, overlooked, or invisible in professional settings.
I have adjusted my communication style, presentation, or personality to be taken seriously.
I have stayed in situations longer than was healthy because leaving felt too risky or uncertain.
I have watched people with less experience or skill advance past me and told myself it was fine.
I feel or have felt that I need to work twice as hard to receive half the recognition.
I have absorbed feedback about my "style" or "presence" that had nothing to do with my results.
I have felt responsible for managing other people's comfort with my ambition, success, or directness.
I have experienced a significant professional setback that I have not fully processed.
I carry the weight of representation — the sense that my performance reflects on a group, not just me.
I have felt the relief of leaving a room and finally being able to exhale.
Readiness Signals

These statements surface your proximity to change — how close you already are to what comes next.

I have started imagining a different version of my professional life, even if I have not said it out loud.
I find myself more interested in conversations about meaning, legacy, or impact than about titles or compensation.
I have had the thought, "I don't think I can keep doing this the way I've been doing it."
I feel more alive in certain conversations, environments, or projects — and I have noticed the contrast.
I have done quiet research: reading, listening, or exploring options without telling anyone.
I know, somewhere, that something needs to change — I am just not sure what it is yet.
I have a skill, perspective, or body of knowledge I have not fully deployed or claimed.
I am more risk-tolerant than I used to be, or more risk-averse — either shift feels significant.
I have caught myself romanticizing what it would feel like to start over or start something new.
I am here, in this space, because part of me already knows it is time.

Check every statement that is true. Then let your score speak.
